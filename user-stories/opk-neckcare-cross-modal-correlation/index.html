User story · Cross-modal correlation

When downward eye movement count in optokinetic (OPK) test meets NeckCare Butterfly scores

At a neuro-optometry and vestibular performance clinic, clinicians already captured rich eye-movement and cervical sensorimotor data. What they lacked was integration across modalities: Cognuro VNG/OPK and NeckCare cervical measures lived in separate workflows and systems, so teams rarely got an integrated signal for clinical reasoning. Clinical Token AI agents connected those modalities with clinician approval at each step, then prepared de-identified evidence for peer discussion on the Evidence Exchange.

Who this story is about

The users are clinicians in a neuro-optometry and vestibular performance setting: doctors and clinical staff who evaluate balance, dizziness, concussion-related symptoms, and visual-vestibular performance. Day to day they order and review eye-movement testing alongside cervical assessments. They are not data engineers. They need the clinic’s own multimodal archive to stay useful without exporting patient identity into a research warehouse.

Two modalities, one patient story

Cognuro: VNG, OPK, and nystagmus metrics

Cognuro provides neurotechnology for objective eye-movement and related measurements. Through VNG-style capture, clinicians record tasks such as gaze, pursuit, saccades, and optokinetics (OPK), including vertical directions. OPK presents moving visual stimuli (for example, striped targets) and measures how the eyes respond. Metrics include nystagmus-like motion patterns and counts that summarize how strongly the oculomotor system follows those stimuli.

NeckCare: range of motion, joint position error, and Butterfly

NeckCare assesses cervical function with objective sensor-based tests. Typical measures include:

  • Range of motion, how far and freely the neck moves.
  • Joint position error (JPE), proprioception: can the patient return the head accurately to a remembered neutral after movement.
  • Butterfly Test, cervical sensorimotor control and movement sense. The patient tracks a moving visual target with head-and-neck motion while a head-worn sensor records accuracy. Scores summarize deviation from the target, time on target, and whether the patient leads or lags. Poorer Butterfly performance is associated with impaired eye-head-neck coordination, often discussed in neck pain, whiplash, concussion, and dizziness contexts.

In other words, Butterfly is not “just flexibility.” It quantifies how well neck sensorimotor control keeps the head synchronized with a dynamic visual path.

How agentic AI helped the clinical workflow

Clinicians stayed in the loop. Clinical Token AI agents proposed plans, waited for approval, then executed. The sequence looked like this:

  1. Inventory. AI agents scanned available clinic data for the relevant modalities, so the team could see what was present before any analysis.
  2. Normalize and project. Different vendor exports and schemas were brought into a shared analysis space so Cognuro and NeckCare metrics could be compared without manual spreadsheet joins.
  3. Cross-connect patients. AI agents identified the cohort that had both Cognuro OPK/VNG measurements and NeckCare cervical measures, the only patients for whom a true cross-modal link is meaningful.
  4. Correlate. Across modalities and metrics (OPK directions and counts, Butterfly and related NeckCare scores, and other cervical measures), AI agents ranked relationships and surfaced the strongest signals for clinician review.

Less Admin. More Care.

That is the practical promise: not replacing clinical judgment, but removing the days of inventory, join logic, and exploratory plotting so clinicians can spend time on interpretation and care planning.

The finding

In the dual-modality cohort, the most significant correlation the AI agents highlighted was between:

  • OPK vertical downward visual stimuli (moving striped targets in the downward direction), associated with higher nystagmoid motion pattern counts on Cognuro eye-movement testing, and
  • worse NeckCare Butterfly scores, indicating poorer cervical sensorimotor tracking performance.

Clinically, that pairing is suggestive: stronger oculomotor response to downward OPK stimulation co-occurring with reduced eye-head-neck coordination on Butterfly. The AI agents did not invent a diagnosis. They made the cohort and the correlation inspectable so clinicians could enlarge the figure, challenge the join, and decide what to do next.
